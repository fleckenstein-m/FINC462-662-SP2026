1   Treasury Bills

Treasury bills (T-bills) are zero-coupon instruments — they pay no periodic interest. Instead, they are issued at a discount and redeemed at par. In the secondary market T-bills are quoted as a discount yield \(y_d\).

\[ P \;=\; 100 \;\times\;\Bigl(1 \;-\; y_d \;\times\; \frac{\text{Days to Maturity}}{360}\Bigr) \]
Day-Count Convention:  T-bill discount yields use actual / 360 — the numerator counts actual calendar days, and the year is assumed to have 360 days.

Worked Example

Security: 26-week T-bill maturing August 6, 2026
Trade date: February 5, 2026  →  Settlement date: February 6, 2026 (T + 1)
Discount yield: \(y_d = 4.210\%\)

2   Treasury Notes and Bonds

Reference Note Used Throughout This Section
  • Coupon: 4.000% (semi-annual; pays May 15 and Nov 15)
  • Issued: November 17, 2025
  • Maturity: November 15, 2030
  • Par value: $100

Part A — Price Quotes and Clean Prices

Treasury note and bond prices are quoted in points (dollars) and 32nds of a point. A quote of 102-25 means 102 points plus 25/32nds. For additional precision, sub-32nd increments are used:

  • A trailing "+" adds 1/64 of a point (i.e., half a 32nd).
  • A trailing fraction (e.g., 3/4) represents fractional 32nds — so 10 3/4 means \(\tfrac{10.75}{32}\).

The clean price (or flat price) is the dollar price per $100 par obtained by converting the quote.

Anatomy of a Treasury Quote
102Points ($) - 25Ticks (32nds) +Sub-tick (1/64)

Example A

Trade date: February 10, 2026  →  Settlement: February 11, 2026
Quote: 102-25+

Clean Price \(= 102 + \dfrac{25}{32} + \dfrac{1}{64} = 102 + 0.78125 + 0.015625 = \$102.7969\)

Example B

Trade date: September 15, 2026  →  Settlement: September 16, 2026
Quote: 101-10 3/4

Clean Price \(= 101 + \dfrac{10.75}{32} = 101 + 0.335938 = \$101.3359\)

Part B — Accrued Interest and the Dirty Price

Between coupon dates the buyer must compensate the seller for interest that has accumulated since the last coupon payment. This compensation is called accrued interest.

Dirty Price  =  Clean Price  +  Accrued Interest

The dirty price (or full price, invoice price) is the actual dollar amount the buyer pays the seller.
